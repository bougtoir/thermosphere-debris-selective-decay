# Post-revision review fixes — audit

Three findings raised on the ASR revision, and what was changed. All numbers
below are regenerated by `make all`; none are hand-entered.

## 1. Column-expansion temperature fraction was first order, not exact

`expansion_temperature_fraction()` returned `ln(1+delta) H / L`, which is the
first-order expansion of the exact condition and understates the required
heating.

For an isothermal hydrostatic layer `rho(z) = rho(z_base) exp(-(z-z_base)/H)`,
scaling T (and hence H) by `1+eps` multiplies the density at z by
`exp[L eps / (H (1+eps))]` with `L = z - z_base`. With
`q = ln(1+delta) H / L`, the condition is `eps/(1+eps) = q`, i.e.

    eps = q / (1 - q)      (q < 1)
    unattainable            (q >= 1)

`q >= 1` means no finite uniform temperature increase of that column reaches
the requested multiplier; the function now returns `inf` and the audit records
`column_attainable = False`.

At 400 km, delta = 5 (column base 120 km, H from the local profile):
`q = 0.386947`, exact `eps = 0.631181` versus the old first-order
`eps = 0.386947` — the old value understated the heating, and the column
energy bound with it, by a factor 1.63. `q_column` and `column_attainable`
are new columns in `energy_bounds_audit.csv` and `q_column` is now shown in
manuscript Table 8. Regression tests: `tests/test_review_fixes.py`
(`test_expansion_fraction_is_exact_not_first_order`,
`test_expansion_fraction_unattainable_when_q_ge_one`) check both the exact
round trip through the hydrostatic relation and the `inf` guard.

## 2. "Tracking" exposure was measured on the unperturbed ephemeris

The tracking arms sampled the enhancement along the *unperturbed* orbit. That
is the perfect-co-location convention: the intervention is assumed to stay
centred on the object however the object moves. It is an upper bound, because
the induced drag itself moves the object relative to a patch centred on the
predicted orbit.

Changes:

* `exposure_class.instrument()` documents the convention and returns
  `ephemeris="unperturbed"`.
* `exposure_class.instrument_decaying()` (new) integrates the semi-major axis
  while the intervention is active and samples the patch along the trajectory
  the drag itself produces; it returns `ephemeris="drag_updated"`.
* `fastprop.decay_lifetime()` now accumulates the secular phases continuously
  across steps (`_rebased()`), takes each step with the midpoint semi-major
  axis, shortens steps overlapping an intervention window to
  `DT_DAY_ACTIVE = 0.01 d`, covers sample windows exactly, and stitches
  encounters that span adjacent active steps so they are not double counted.
* `audit_gap_decomposition.py` reports both arms separately:
  `tracking_bound_perfect` (unperturbed) and `tracking_simulated`
  (drag-updated), plus a co-location retention factor and the closure of the
  factorization against the simulated arm.
* `audit_matched_exposure.py` measures every arm along the drag-updated
  trajectory.
* The manuscript reports both values and never calls the unperturbed value
  simply "tracking".

Case A, 1 day, delta = 5, sigma_h = 200 km:

| arm | ephemeris | extra dv (m/s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fixed pulse (1 event) | unperturbed | 3.979e-3 |
| tracking, perfect co-location bound | unperturbed | 6.8695 |
| tracking, drift-aware | drag_updated | 3.5303 |

Co-location retention 0.5139; the perfect-bound/pulse ratio is 1726x and the
drift-aware/pulse ratio 887x, reproduced by the exposure factorization times
the retention to within 1.5%.

Because the drift is driven by the induced decay, the drift-aware value
depends on the integration step. `audit_orbit_convergence.py` (new) runs the
ladder `dt_active = 0.25 ... 0.002 d`, writes
`results/tables/orbit_step_convergence.csv`, and shows second-order
convergence with the production step (0.01 d) within 1e-4 of the finest rung.
`tests/test_review_fixes.py::test_active_step_refinement_converges` locks this
in, and `test_drift_aware_exposure_below_perfect_bound` locks in the ordering
of the two conventions.

Scope unchanged: the perfect bound is a mathematical limit on co-location
strategies, the drift-aware value is the same limit evaluated with the target
moving under the drag the intervention induces, and fully self-consistent
orbit/atmosphere coupling (feedback on the density field, re-aiming control
laws) remains outside scope and is stated as such.

## 3. Non-physical selectivity ratios appeared as numbers

Selectivity ratios were tabulated even where the denominator was a Gaussian
tail (~50 sigma), below numerical resolution, or a non-encounter — the source
of the old 1e80-scale "selectivity". `phase7_selectivity.csv` already carried
`ratio_status` / `ratio_meaningful`; the manuscript now honours them:

* Table 3 prints `undefined` instead of the numeral wherever
  `ratio_meaningful` is false, and keeps `ratio_status` alongside so the
  reason is visible.
* `build_manuscript.meaningful_ratio()` raises and fails the build if a prose
  value would be taken from a non-physical ratio.
* Absolute target and protected delta-v and the exposure classes are reported
  unchanged, so the collateral suppression is still quantified — as an
  absolute number rather than as a ratio against a tail.

## 4. Integrator bookkeeping at the horizon, at reentry, and across windows

Three further review findings on `fastprop.decay_lifetime()`, all bookkeeping
rather than physics, but all able to leak into reported numbers:

* **Horizon overrun.** Steps were always `dt_day` long, so an object that did
  not reenter was integrated past `t_max_s` by up to one step and its
  right-censored lifetime was reported at that later time. The step is now
  `min(dt_day * DAY, t_max_s - t)`, so a surviving object's lifetime is the
  requested horizon exactly (`test_integration_stops_at_requested_horizon`).
* **Drag accrued after reentry.** When reentry happened inside a step, the
  exposure and the extra impulse had already been integrated over the whole
  step while only the time was truncated at the reentry instant, so
  `extra_drag_impulse_ms` and `exposure_window_s` could describe a longer
  interval than the returned lifetime. A step that reaches reentry is now
  retaken over the shortened interval that ends at reentry, and only that
  interval is committed to the exposure aggregates
  (`test_reentry_truncates_exposure_and_impulse`).
* **Encounters merged across a gap between windows.** The `was_inside` flag
  that stitches a patch crossing spanning a step boundary into one encounter
  was also carried across the inactive time between two disjoint intervention
  windows, merging one encounter per window into one. Stitching now applies
  only when the two exposure intervals are contiguous
  (`test_disjoint_windows_count_separate_encounters`).

The production results are insensitive to the first two (no run reaches
reentry inside its horizon at the reported grid, and the gap-decomposition and
matched-exposure values are unchanged to all printed digits); the third
affects campaign runs with repeated windows, whose encounter counts are
regenerated here.
