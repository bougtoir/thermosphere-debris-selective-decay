# Transport-boundary audit: provenance of the "41%" (Phase 4)

Addresses hostile-review items **M1** (41% presented as a probability) and
**M2** (unsourced diffusivity and wind ranges).

Generated by `scripts/audit_transport_boundary.py` ->
`results/tables/transport_boundary_sensitivity.csv`.

## What the frozen number actually is

`scripts/phase12_classification.py` builds a full product grid

    regime {low, moderate, high, storm} x altitude {250,300,400,500,600,750} km
    x delta {1,5,20} x sigma_h {50,200,1000} km
    x kappa_h {1e3,1e5,1e6} m^2/s x u {0,100,300} m/s

and flags a row survivable when

    tau_diff = sigma_h^2 / (2 kappa_h) >= 3600 s   AND
    tau_adv  = sigma_h / u            >= 3600 s   (tau_adv = inf for u = 0)

Neither criterion depends on regime, altitude or delta, so the reported
fraction is entirely determined by the 27 (sigma_h, kappa, u) combinations:
11 of 27 survive = **40.7%**, rounded to 41% in the frozen manuscript.

**It is a fraction of a hand-chosen design grid, not a probability.** No
distribution over kappa or u was specified or sampled, the grid points are
not equally likely, and two of the three wind values (0 m/s) and one of the
three sigma values (1000 km) are not representative of the case the paper
cares about. Every occurrence of probabilistic wording ("probability",
"plausible combinations", "likely") is replaced by "of the surveyed parameter
combinations", and the headline is replaced by the conditional statement
below.

## Sensitivity to the grid and the criterion

`transport_boundary_sensitivity.csv` recomputes the surviving fraction for
3 sigma-grids x 3 kappa-grids x 4 wind-grids x 4 survival criteria
(0.5, 1, 3, 6 h). At the 1 h criterion the surviving fraction ranges from
**8.3% to 55.6%** depending only on grid choice - i.e. the headline number is
almost as sensitive to the sweep design as to the physics. This is exactly
why it cannot be quoted as a probability.

Two restrictions make the result robust:

| restriction | why it matters | surviving fraction at 1 h |
|---|---|---|
| sigma_h <= 200 km (patch small enough to be selective) | a 1000 km patch covers ~10% of an orbital circumference; the "selectivity" it provides is meaningless | 27.8% (frozen grid); <= 33% over all variants |
| sigma_h <= 200 km AND u >= 100 m/s (any realistic mean wind) | the thermosphere is never windless at these altitudes | **0% in every grid variant tested** |

At u >= 50 m/s the maximum over all variants is 25%, attained only by the
combination sigma_h = 200 km with u = 50 m/s (tau_adv = 4000 s, marginally
above the 1 h criterion). The boundary is therefore sharp and is set by
advection, not diffusion: for a selective patch, horizontal wind flushes the
enhanced volume in sigma_h/u ~ 200 km / 100 m/s ~ 33 min.

## Provenance of the parameter ranges

| parameter | range used | basis |
|---|---|---|
| horizontal effective diffusivity kappa_h | 1e3 - 1e6 m^2/s | spans molecular-diffusion-dominated horizontal spreading above the turbopause through large-scale eddy/wave mixing estimates; the wide range is deliberately agnostic and the result is shown to be insensitive to it (advection dominates) |
| horizontal neutral wind u | 0 - 300 m/s (0 retained only for the frozen-grid reproduction; 50 - 600 m/s in the variants) | climatological thermospheric neutral winds are tens to ~200 m/s under quiet conditions and several hundred m/s at high latitudes during geomagnetic activity (HWM-class empirical wind models) |
| patch vertical scale sigma_v | 30 km | comparable to the local density scale height at 300-500 km; a thinner patch is not dynamically sustainable, a thicker one dilutes the enhancement |
| survival criterion | 0.5 - 6 h | set by the orbital revisit geometry: the target returns to a given ground-fixed region roughly every 90 min, so a patch must persist for at least one revisit to be used more than once |

References for the wind and diffusivity ranges are listed in
`references/references_verified.csv` and are cited at the point of use in the
manuscript Methods; each citation is checked in the Phase-17 traceability
pass. No numerical value in the table above is used outside the range its
citation supports.

## Statement adopted in the revised manuscript

> Of the 27 surveyed (sigma_h, kappa_h, u) combinations, 11 (41%) allow a
> patch to persist for one hour; this is the fraction of a design grid, not a
> probability. The fraction ranges from 8% to 56% across reasonable
> alternative grids. Restricting to patches small enough to be selective
> (sigma_h <= 200 km) in the presence of any realistic mean wind
> (u >= 100 m/s), **no surveyed combination survives one hour**: the
> transport boundary is set by advective flushing on a timescale
> sigma_h/u ~ 30 min.
