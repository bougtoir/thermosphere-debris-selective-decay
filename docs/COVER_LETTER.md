# Cover letter (draft)

Dear Editors,

We submit "Quantifying the feasibility boundary of localized
thermospheric density enhancement for selective orbital debris decay"
for consideration in Advances in Space Research.

The paper asks whether a spatially and temporally localized
thermospheric density increase could selectively accelerate the orbital
decay of low Earth orbit debris while limiting collateral drag on
protected spacecraft — and treats it as a boundary problem, not an
engineering proposal. Its principal contribution is a quantitatively
bounded negative result: even under an idealized sustained on-target
enhancement bound, effects are mm/s–cm/s per day, while every
physically realizable static or advected patch is transport-limited to
negligible per-event effect. Localization is shown to be necessary for
collateral control but incapable of creating the effect itself, and
selectivity provably fails for co-orbital and track-crossing
spacecraft.

All results regenerate from a fully open pipeline (`make all` /
`make quick`); every number in the manuscript is produced
programmatically from the included result tables. Only synthetic
objects are studied; no operational spacecraft are targeted, and no
deployment hardware or procedures are proposed — the study is a
dual-use-conscious feasibility boundary analysis.

The manuscript is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and all
data and code are available in the accompanying repository.

Sincerely,
The authors
