# Gate 1B — Incremental novelty map (pre-results version)

Written before any perturbed GITM output was inspected; Section "Outcome" for
each candidate is completed in Gate 5 (`docs/SCIENTIFIC_CONTRIBUTION_GATE.md`).

## C1 — Physical-response boundary
* Known: localized natural heating creates δ up to ~1 at 400 km (Lühr 2004;
  Carlson 2012); GITM gives ~14 % for a 50 GW auroral pulse (Liu 2015).
* Unknown: whether a compact, mid-latitude, energy-prescribed source produces
  and *keeps* the structures the baseline prescribed (δ = 0.01–100, σh =
  10–3000 km, static or slowly spreading, τ = 600–3600 s).
* Would be new: the density response per joule, its persistence and spread for
  such a source, set against the baseline's prescribed parameter ranges.
* Evidence needed: Gate 3 paired runs (PR1–PR8), energy closure (V8/V9), grid
  convergence.
* Why it matters: fixes the physically reachable part of the baseline's
  parameter space.

## C2 — Coupled response boundary
* Known: storm-enhanced density increases drag and can cause reentry (Fang
  2022; Oliveira 2019).
* Unknown: how much the orbit-integrated drag from a self-consistent localized
  anomaly differs from a prescribed Gaussian with the same peak/width.
* Would be new: the GITM/prescribed drag ratio (PR11) with uncertainty.

## C3 — Selectivity boundary
* Known: nothing quantitative found (Prior-knowledge matrix #6).
* Would be new: target/non-target extra-Δv ratio for self-consistent vs
  prescribed fields (PR10).
* Why it matters: selectivity was the baseline's central concept.

## C4 — Scaling and generalization
* Known: Δv ∝ ∫ρ v²/(2B) dt (tautological); the baseline's scaling fit is a
  restatement of the drag law.
* Would be new only if a non-trivial dependence (energy, deposition altitude,
  linearity) emerges that is not implied by the drag law (PR2, PR7).

## C5 — Quantitative exclusion criteria
* Known: the baseline's prescribed-field exclusion boundary.
* Would be new: an energy-anchored exclusion criterion, e.g. the power needed
  for δ ≥ 1 at 400 km over σh ~ 1000 km, compared with natural storm/cusp
  energy inputs (PR12).

Expected ranking before results: C3 and C5 are the most plausible new coupled
results; C1 is an extension; C2 is method-dependent; C4 is unlikely.
